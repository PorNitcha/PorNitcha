Read this
The August softness is volume, not price. Ready is tracking -10% on value and -11% on volume against July, while realisation holds at 259 THB/kg versus 258 in July. Nothing has been given away on price — fewer kilos moved.
Structure
All of Ready’s growth is RTC, and half of Ready is one customer. Since January 2025 RTC has gone from 8.4 to 23.8 mn (182%) while RTE has shrunk from 27.3 to 23.7 mn. 7-Eleven is 48% of Ready this month and 48% in July — and it is the lowest-margin outlet in the category.

1 · RTE is flat to down, RTC is the whole growth story

Monthly NIV, THB mn, split by format. Stack height is total Ready. The August landing estimate is 42.6 mn.
Good
RTC volume has gone from 39 t to 105 t a month — 167% — and it earns a higher margin than RTE wherever both sell — in GT Dry, RTC runs 47% against RTE’s 35%, and at 7-Eleven 22% against 12%. The pouch format is doing exactly what a good NPD should do.
Watch
RTE peaked at 29.8 mn (Jun'25) and has not been back. Its August landing of 17.5 mn is below every month since Jan'26. RTE is where the cup format lives — if the Big Cup lines are the intended replacement, they are only 4.2 mn MTD and need to be tracked as a stated plan, not as a residual in CHECK.

3 · Why the monthly margin looks broken — it is a quarterly accrual

Trade spend ≥ 20% of NIVbelow 20%credit / reversal7-Eleven Ready: TA as % of NIV, by month
Finding
7-Eleven trade spend lands in quarter-end months, not evenly. Look at the pattern: Mar’25 27%, Jun’25 29%, Sep’25 33%, then Feb–Mar’26 26–28% and Jun’26 31%, against 1–5% in most first months of a quarter and outright credits in Nov’25 (-20%), Jan’26 (-7%) and Apr’26 (-3%). Monthly Ready margin for 7-Eleven is an accrual artefact. Read it quarterly or not at all.

The real number
On a quarterly basis the picture is consistent and uncomfortable: everything except 7-Eleven earns 36–36% and is improving, while 7-Eleven earns 20–35% — a gap of 10 points in the latest quarter. Because 7-Eleven is roughly half the category, blended Ready margin is essentially a 7-Eleven number. Growing Ready through 7-Eleven grows revenue and dilutes margin at the same time.

6 · What to do

  1. Stop reading Ready margin monthly. 7-Eleven trade spend accrues at quarter end, which makes any single month meaningless — the swing is 4% to 44%. Move the Ready margin review to a quarterly cadence, or get the TA accrual spread evenly. This is a reporting fix worth having before the next BU review.
  2. Put a margin floor on 7-Eleven Ready before you chase more volume there. It is half the category at roughly 25% versus 36% everywhere else. Every incremental point of 7-Eleven mix costs blended Ready margin, so growth there needs to be a deliberate choice with a stated payback, not a default.
  3. Give Big C and Makro more Ready. Big C is running 4.8 mn on landing against 2.3 mn in July at the lightest discount in MT; Makro carries the best margin in the channel. Both are small enough that a listing or promo shift moves them materially.
  4. Decide what replaces RTE. RTE has been flat to declining for 19 months while RTC tripled. The Big Cup lines are the apparent answer but sit at 4.2 mn MTD, unbudgeted and parked in the dashboard’s CHECK column. Either give them a target and a category line, or accept RTE as a managed decline.
  5. Ask why August volume is down 11% when price is flat. Realisation is unchanged, so this is orders, not pricing — most likely 7-Eleven order timing given its weight. Worth a call before month-end rather than an explanation after it.

Basis and caveats

Two data bases, deliberately. Monthly volume, NIV, trade spend, net sales and gross profit come from Thaiwah_Channel_PnL_by_Category_por.xlsx (master, P&L basis, actuals to Jul’26). August month-to-date value, volume, realisation, invoice discount, variant and SKU detail come from the 21,277-line billing extract in Daily Sales Dashboard (2026 Food BU)_ 1-24 Aug 2026.xlsb. For Ready the two agree closely — the product is dry, so billed kilos and the master’s dry-basis tonnes are near-identical, and July realisation reconciles at 258 THB/kg on the master against 259 on August billing.

Scope. “Ready” here is RTE + RTC + the Crystal Ready NPD lines (Ready Phrik Khaw and the two Big Cup variants) that the source dashboard leaves in its CHECK column. The dashboard’s own RTE and RTC figures exclude those lines, which is why RTE and RTC individually look weaker there than the category really is. The category total reconciles either way.

Landing estimate = MTD ÷ 20 billing days × 26 billing days in August. Sundays excluded; 12 August kept in. Linear, no seasonality.

Margin figures are gross margin = gross profit ÷ net sales, from the master file, and are July 2026 actuals unless a quarter is named. Trade spend (“TA”) is Trade Agreement + Loyalty + Margin Intra as defined in the master file, expressed as a percentage of NIV. Invoice discount in the MTD tables is a different and additive thing: the ZD-series discount on the billing document itself, before any trade agreement.
