Read this
Domestic is ahead of plan. The whole shortfall is Export. On the 1–23 run rate, Domestic lands at 194.2 mn against a 190.7 mn target — 3.5 mn over. Export lands at 14.6 mn against 33.8 mn — -19.2 mn under. Net: -15.7 mn.
The range
Export is shipment-timed, not daily-linear, so the run rate understates it. If Export simply repeats last August (25.8 mn), Food BU lands at 219.9 mn (98% of target). Hitting 224.5 mn needs Export to finish at 30.3 mn — 19.6 mn in the remaining 7 billing days, against 10.7 mn in the first 19. It has cleared a 30-mn-plus month four times in 2026, so this is a timing question, not a capability one.

8 · What 24 August added

For continuity with the dashboard Panawan circulated on 24 August.
One extra day
24 August (a Monday) added 5.4 mn, taking MTD from 152.6 to 158.0 mn and % to target from 68.0% to 70.4%. Because 5.4 mn is well below the 8.0 mn average billing day, the landing estimate falls from 208.8 to 205.4 mn. Nothing structural changed — one soft day simply pulled the run rate down.
Where it landed
Almost all of it in three channels: GTF 0.5, MT 1.8, GTD 1.2, plus IX 1.6 and IC 0.3 mn. Export billed nothing on 24 August — still 10.7 mn for the month. That is the number to watch, not the total.

Basis, method and caveats

Why this is derived, not downloaded. There is no 1–23 August dashboard — Panawan’s emails jump from 1–21 to 1–24 August. This cut is therefore rebuilt from the raw SAP billing extract inside the 1–24 August workbook, filtered to billing dates on or before 23 August.

The rebuild is validated, not approximated. Applying the same logic to the full 1–24 period reproduces the published dashboard exactly: 78 of 78 entities match to the satang — every channel, sub-channel, salesman code and MT customer — and all 14 category buckets match exactly, including the dashboard’s own CHECK residual of 4,283,943. Three source conventions had to be reverse-engineered to get there and are worth knowing: RTC is the five Ready pouch lines only; RTE excludes Ready Phrik Khaw and the two Big Cup lines (they sit in CHECK); and four van sub-codes (VCM7, VNR4, VRE5, VSR6) report into WSF codes TF2, TF4, TF4 and TF5 rather than into their own van routes.

Targets, LE, AP and LY are month-level figures taken unchanged from the dashboard’s Data sheet, so they are identical to the 1–24 cut. LY is full-month August 2025, not the same 23-day window — so the landing estimate, not the MTD, is the fair thing to compare against it, and that is what the “Landing % LY” column does.

Landing estimate = MTD ÷ 19 billing days elapsed × 26 billing days in August. Sundays are excluded; 12 August (Mother’s Day) is kept in because it still billed 3.7 mn. It is a linear extrapolation with no seasonality or month-end push, and it is least reliable for Export, ASN and IX where a single shipment moves the month. Treat Food BU’s landing as a band of roughly 205–212 mn on domestic run rate alone.

“Cust. billed” is my own count of distinct bill-to parties, not the dashboard’s active-customer measure. The two are on different definitions — the dashboard shows 615 for Food BU on 24 August against a target of 4,573 — so I have not tried to reproduce its figure and would not read anything into the target until the definition is confirmed.
