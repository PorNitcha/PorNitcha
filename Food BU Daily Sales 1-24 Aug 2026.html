The one thing to know

Domestic is on pace; the whole shortfall is Export. Food BU has billed THB 158.0 mn against an August target of 224.5 mn — 70.4% achieved with 76.9% of the working month gone. Extrapolating the current daily run rate, the month lands at roughly 205 mn (92% of target, gap -19.1 mn).

Split it and the picture changes completely: Food Domestic lands at ~192 mn vs a 191 mn target — 100%, effectively on plan. Food Export lands at ~13.9 mn vs 33.8 mn, a -19.9 mn hole. That single number is larger than the whole Food BU gap of -19.1 mn — the other eight channels net together to +0.9 mn.

Export is shipment-timed, not daily-linear, so a run-rate read understates it. If Export merely repeats last August (25.8 mn), Food BU lands at ~217 mn (97% of target). That range — 205 to 217 mn — is the real question for the month, and it is answered in the Export shipping schedule, not in the sales force.

Scenario range for August. Domestic run rate holds (192 mn) and Export lands where it is tracking (13.9 mn) ⇒ 205 mn, 92% of target. Domestic holds and Export repeats LY (25.8 mn) ⇒ 217 mn, 97%. Hitting the 224.5 mn target needs Export to finish the month at 32.9 mn — i.e. 22.3 mn billed in the 6 working days that remain, against 10.7 mn in the first 20. A 32.9 mn export month is not unusual in itself — Export cleared that level in four of the seven closed months of 2026 (Jan 32.3, Mar 40.5, May 32.9, Jul 37.9 mn) — so the question is shipment timing, not capability.

8 · What to do with this

  1. Get the Export shipping schedule for 25–31 August before you accept the 205 mn landing. The difference between the low and high case for Food BU is 11.9 mn of export timing and nothing else. Panawan’s own note on the email — that SAP does not yet carry the invoices — suggests part of this is a posting lag rather than lost volume. Confirm which.
  2. Re-map the export target off FTHEX01. As the file stands, the export team cannot see its own achievement and the channel looks worse at salesman level than it is. This is a one-line fix in the source workbook.
  3. Take GTD1 to the ASM. Five codes below 50% with 6 working days left; TDCE2 at 21% and TDCC1 at 31% are the two to open with. GTD1’s 12.6 mn target versus 11.1 mn LY is not the issue — the codes are also running well below their own last-August levels.
  4. Protect the MT upside. MT is the one large channel likely to beat target (2.6 mn ahead). Big C, TFF Mart and 7-Eleven are carrying it; make sure the late-August order book for those three is loaded, because MT is what is currently masking GTD and IC.
  5. Reconcile the active-customer definition before the next dashboard, so the AC column becomes usable. As it stands it is the only part of this file I would not put in front of the BU head.
  6. Watch Ready. RTE + RTC tracking ~10% below July run rate is small in absolute terms this month but it is the category the 2026 plan leans on. One month is not a trend; two would be.

Reconciliation performed. The 21,277 billing lines sum to THB 158,004,202, matching the dashboard MTD to the satang, and each of the nine channels reconciles independently from Sales Group. The 13 core categories sum to 153,720,259, leaving 4,283,943 in unmapped Crystal Ready / NPD lines — the dashboard’s own CHECK column. Master-file category NIV for July 2026 sums to 230.32 mn against the dashboard’s 230,310,897, and master Aug’25 NIV of 211.36 mn equals the dashboard LY figure exactly, so the two files are on the same basis.

Time-gone conventions differ — on purpose. The source dashboard reports time gone as 79.3% (23 of 29 days), which appears to exclude public holidays but not Sundays. This analysis uses two explicit measures instead: calendar 77.4% (24 of 31 days), used for the traffic lights, and billing days 76.9% (20 of 26, Sundays excluded), used for the landing estimates. All three land within 2.4 points, so no conclusion here turns on the choice — but the numbers will not tie to the source header, and that is why.

Basis notes. Sales are net value after discount (NIV), THB. “LY” in the source dashboard is full-month August 2025, not the same 24-day period — so the dashboard’s own “% vs LY” compares 24 days against 31 and reads low by construction. This analysis uses the landing estimate for LY comparison instead. Landing estimates are a linear working-day extrapolation and carry no seasonality or month-end-push adjustment; they are least reliable for Export, ASN and IX, where single shipments move the month.
